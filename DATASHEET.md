# Datasheet for TokWatt v0.1.0

Structured after *Datasheets for Datasets* (Gebru et al., 2021).

## Motivation

**Why was the dataset created?** Electricity supply has become a binding constraint on AI deployment, yet the public evidence on the energy of individual AI tasks is scattered across company blogs, preprints, benchmarks and journalism, and published numbers are rarely comparable. TokWatt makes the evidence comparable by recording each estimate with its measurement boundary and functional unit, and by publishing reproducible harmonized reference values per task.

**Who created it?** Abderaouf Bahi, LIMA Laboratory (a.bahi@univ-eltarf.dz).

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?** Each row of `estimates.csv` is one published estimate of the energy (Wh) for one functional unit of AI inference (a query, a session, an image, a clip, an inference of a classic ML task), from one source.

**How many instances?** 134 estimates from 13 sources (22 sources in total including macro, grid, token-factor and reference data); 44 estimates form the reference sets behind `task_defaults.csv`; 31 macro indicators; 220 grid-carbon rows; 6 token factors; 10 everyday equivalents.

**Is it a sample?** It is a curated selection, not exhaustive. v0.1.0 prioritises widely cited, first-hand sources from 2023–2026 that report a functional unit and enough method detail to classify the boundary.

**Missing information.** Many sources do not report intervals, token counts, hardware, or emissions; those cells are blank. No closed developer other than Google, OpenAI and Mistral has published per-query figures in the sources reviewed.

**Errors and noise.** Values were transcribed from the sources; the `notes` column records inconsistencies found inside sources (e.g. abstract vs body values). Modeled estimates inherit their authors' assumptions.

**Confidential or personal data?** None.

## Collection process

Sources were identified by web search and citation chaining between 25 October 2026 and the release date, read in full where accessible, and values were transcribed with their locator (table, figure or section).

**AI assistance (disclosure).** Initial identification of sources and extraction of values were performed with an AI assistant (Claude, Anthropic). The author then checked every value against its cited locator; all rows carry `verification = human_verified`. New rows are checked the same way before release. All responsibility for the content rests with the author.

## Preprocessing

Unit conversion to Wh; per-1,000-inference values divided accordingly; joules divided by 3,600; US gallons to mL (× 3,785.41). Derived columns (`harmonization_factor`, `energy_wh_harmonized`) and `task_defaults.csv` are generated by `scripts/build.py` and checked by `scripts/validate.py`. Grid-carbon rows keep the latest year per country from Our World in Data, dropping years before 2022.

## Uses

**Intended.** Comparing published estimates on a like-for-like basis; scenario analysis of AI electricity demand; teaching; calculators that need sourced per-task values with uncertainty; meta-research on measurement practice.

**Not intended.** Ranking named commercial products as if the numbers were audited measurements; regulatory compliance reporting; claims about a specific data centre's emissions.

## Distribution

GitHub (primary; one tagged release per version), with the calculator on GitHub Pages. Data CC BY 4.0; code MIT.

## Maintenance

Versioned with semantic versioning; every release is listed in `CHANGELOG.md`. New estimates are welcome through pull requests meeting the requirements in `README.md`. Errata are fixed in a patch release and noted in the changelog.
