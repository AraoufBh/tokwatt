Title:
TokWatt: an open, boundary-annotated database of AI inference energy, from chat answers to agents

Authors:
Abderaouf Bahi

Abstract (1550 characters; arXiv limit 1,920):
Electricity supply now limits how fast artificial intelligence (AI) can be deployed, yet the public evidence on how much energy one AI task uses is scattered and hard to compare. Published figures for a single chatbot answer differ by two orders of magnitude, mostly because studies count different parts of the system and measure different units of work. We present TokWatt, an open database of 134 published estimates of AI inference energy from 13 sources (2023-2026), covering 20 tasks from classic machine-learning inferences to chat answers, reasoning, long-document queries, image and video generation and coding agents. Each estimate records its functional unit, five measurement-boundary components, method, reported interval and the location of the value in its source. A reproducible procedure scales estimates to a common server-plus-facility boundary and derives per-task reference values with ranges. The reference values span six orders of magnitude: 0.34 Wh for a short chat answer (range 0.16-0.99), 4.7 Wh for a reasoning answer, 124 Wh for a short video clip and 150 Wh for one task delegated to a coding agent. Five independent estimates for default production chat systems agree within a factor of 1.8, and the reference values move by at most 12% under alternative harmonization parameters. A simple identity links personal use to grid load: E watt-hours per person per day across one billion people is an average draw of E/24 gigawatts. The data (CC BY 4.0), code, datasheet and a browser-based calculator are openly available.
